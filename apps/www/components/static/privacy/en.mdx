{props.data.PLATFORM_NAME} processes data about people's religious lives. Such data is sensitive in law. This document explains what is collected, why, for how long, and what rights you can exercise.

## 1. Who processes your data

This depends on the type of data.

**{props.data.OPERATOR_LEGAL_NAME}**, {props.data.OPERATOR_ADDRESS}, is the controller for data it collects on its own behalf: user accounts, connection logs, audience measurement, billing.

**Each parish, deanery or diocese using the Platform** is the controller for the data it places in its space: sacramental registers, member directories, published content. {props.data.OPERATOR_SHORT_NAME} acts only as a processor in that respect.

In practice: for a question about your baptismal record, contact the parish concerned. For a question about your account, contact {props.data.OPERATOR_SHORT_NAME}.

## 3. Data processed

### 3.1 Account data

Surname, first name, email address, hashed password, profile photograph where applicable, associated parish, assigned role, display language.

Where you authenticate through Google, Facebook or LinkedIn: the provider's identifier, plus the name, email address and photograph it transmits. No password from those services is received or stored.

### 3.2 Sacramental register data

Surname and first names, date and place of birth, date and place of celebration, parentage, godparents, the minister of the sacrament, later marginal entries (confirmation, marriage, ordination, death), record number and register references.

This data reveals religious affiliation. It constitutes **sensitive data** under {props.data.SENSITIVE_DATA_PROVISIONS}, which in principle prohibits processing data revealing religious beliefs outside statutory exceptions.

Processing rests on the following grounds: it is carried out by a body with a religious aim, in the course of its legitimate activities, with appropriate safeguards, and relates solely to its members or to persons in regular contact with it; the data is not disclosed to outside third parties without the data subjects' consent. It also meets the canonical obligation to keep parish registers (canons 535 and 877 of the Code of Canon Law).

### 3.3 Operational data

IP address, timestamps, pages visited, browser and device type, error and security logs.

### 3.4 Uploaded content

Photographs, sound recordings, scanned documents, announcement texts, minutes of meetings and celebrations.

Images and recordings featuring identifiable people are published only with their agreement, obtained by the user entity.

## 7. Retention periods

| Data | Period |
|---|---|
| User account | Duration of activity, then 12 months |
| Connection logs | 12 months |
| Sacramental registers | **Retained permanently** |
| Published content | Until withdrawn by the user entity |
| Accounting records | 10 years |
| Backups | Rolling 90 days |

Permanent retention of registers needs explaining. A baptismal record is an ecclesiastical civil-status record whose evidential value has no expiry: it is required throughout a person's life for admission to other sacraments, and after death it retains archival, genealogical and historical value. Canon law requires it to be kept in perpetuity. {props.data.ARCHIVAL_JUSTIFICATION}

The right to erasure does not extend to these registers. A person may instead request a marginal entry, notably in the case of a formal act of defection from the Catholic Church: the record is not deleted, and the entry is noted in the margin.

## 8. Deceased persons

A substantial share of the digitised records concerns people who have died, some of them decades ago. {props.data.DATA_PROTECTION_LAW_SHORT} and the GDPR protect living natural persons; they do not apply to the deceased.

These records are nonetheless handled carefully, because they reveal information about living people — parentage, kinship, family religious affiliation. Public dissemination of recent records is restricted, and a closure period applies before any wider release for genealogical purposes.

The heirs of a deceased person may request correction of manifestly erroneous information concerning them.

## 9. Minors

The registers contain data about minors, since baptism is usually conferred in early childhood. This data is collected from parents or the holder of parental authority at the time of the celebration.

Records concerning minors, or persons who have recently reached majority, are not made publicly viewable. Access is limited to persons authorised by the parish and to the data subject or their legal representatives.

Account registration is subject to the age conditions set out in the Terms of Service.

## 10. Security

The following measures are in place: encrypted communications (TLS), passwords hashed with a one-way derivation function, logical partitioning of spaces, role- and policy-based access control, logging of register access, regular encrypted backups, restricted and monitored administrator access, network filtering and intrusion detection.

In the event of a data breach likely to create a risk to individuals, the publisher informs {props.data.SUPERVISORY_AUTHORITY_SHORT} and the user entities concerned as soon as possible, and the data subjects where the risk is high.

## 11. Your rights

You have rights of access, rectification, objection, restriction, erasure within the limits set out in section 7, and portability for data you have provided.

For account data: {props.data.CONTACT_EMAIL}.
For register data: contact the parish concerned, whose details appear on its space.

A response is provided within thirty days at most. Proof of identity may be requested where there is reasonable doubt as to who is making the request.

You may lodge a complaint with {props.data.SUPERVISORY_AUTHORITY}.
